# Module and quotient authoring profile v2

`typed-commalg-modules-v2` adds quotient modules, canonical linear maps, finite generation,
Nakayama's lemma, tensor products and flatness to a new, isolated OpenMath language. It
does not change any v1 registry, validator, manifest or interpretation. This profile is
for local authoring, validation and storage; registration alone does not establish serving,
model structuring, selection quality, publication or runtime proof reuse.

The profile has 22 original general theorem cards. Every card has a matching Lean example
compiled in the existing pinned workspace by `generate-commalg-modules-v2-batch.py` before
the generator writes its JSON. The batch envelope includes hashes of the entire exact
Lean bundle, validator and registry, plus the actual toolchain and Mathlib revision.
Compilation verifies the Lean targets; review of the natural statement and OpenMath is
a separate semantic obligation. No `sorry`, `admit`, new axioms or compiler-result fixtures
are used.

## Mathematical structures and dependent identity

The typed cdbase is `urn:pals:openmath:typed-commalg-modules:v2`, with mathematical symbols
in `module2` and typed binders in `typed1`. Standard logical and equality symbols require
the explicit `http://www.openmath.org/cd` cdbase. The registry pins every symbol's signature,
argument order and Lean meaning. Its symbol set cannot extend the executable validator.

| Declaration | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `CommRing` | An arbitrary type with `CommRing R`. |
| `LocalRing` | An arbitrary type with `CommRing R` and `IsLocalRing R`. |
| `Module(R)` | An additive commutative group with its specified `Module R M` structure. |
| `Ideal(R)` | An ideal of the previously declared exact ring. |
| `Submodule(R,M)` | A submodule of the exact previously declared or constructed module. |
| `LinearMap(R,M,N)` | An R-linear map with the exact indicated source and target. |

`quotient(R,M,U)` constructs the module `M ⧸ U`. It is a carrier expression, not a new
arbitrary `Module(R)` binder. Its identity includes R, M and the full validated structural
term U. `submodule_carrier(R,M,U)` constructs the induced module on U. `tensor(R,M,N)`
constructs `TensorProduct R M N` with ordered factors. These constructed carriers can be
used in subsequent dependent sorts, including bound maps out of a quotient. A forged
`QuotientModule` sort, undeclared operand, different submodule, different ring, or swapped
tensor factor is rejected. No automatic equality-of-carriers, isomorphism coercion or
carrier erasure occurs.

## Operators and ordering

The JSON registry is the complete signature reference. In particular:

- `quotient_map(R,M,U)` denotes `U.mkQ : M →ₗ[R] M ⧸ U`.
- `inclusion(R,M,U)` denotes `U.subtype : U →ₗ[R] M`.
- `compose(R,M,N,L,f,g)` denotes **`g.comp f`**: first f, then g.
- `kernel(R,M,N,f)` is a submodule of M; `range(R,M,N,f)` is a submodule of N.
- `map(R,M,N,f,U)` and `comap(R,M,N,f,V)` have source U⊆M and target V⊆N respectively.
- `exact(R,M,N,L,f,g)` means `Function.Exact f g`, equivalently `range f = ker g`.
  It does not imply injectivity of f or surjectivity of g.
- `tensor_map_left(R,X,M,N,f)` denotes `f.lTensor X = identity_X ⊗ f` from X⊗M to X⊗N.
- `finite(R,M)` means `Module.Finite R M`, i.e. finite generation, **not finite cardinality**.
- `fg(R,M,U)` means `U.FG`; `finite(R,submodule_carrier(R,M,U))` is its equivalent
  induced-module formulation.
- `flat(R,M)` means `Module.Flat R M`, the mathematical flatness property; it does not
  assert that M is free, Noetherian or finitely generated.
- `module_zero(R,M)` means `Subsingleton M`; the emitted Nakayama cards instead state
  the equivalent explicit equality `(⊤ : Submodule R M) = ⊥`.
- `ideal_smul(R,M,I,U)` is `I • U`, the submodule generated by scalar products of elements
  of I and U. Thus `ideal_smul(R,M,I,submodule_top(R,M))` means IM. It is not multiplication
  by a chosen element of I and it does not refer to cardinality.
- `ideal_jacobson(R,I)` denotes `Ideal.jacobson I`; `ideal_jacobson(R,ideal_bot(R))`
  is the Jacobson radical of R.
- `maximal_ideal(R)` is permitted only for an explicit `LocalRing` binding and denotes
  `IsLocalRing.maximalIdeal R`. It cannot be applied to a merely commutative ring.

Equality is available for same-sort ideals, submodules and linear maps, not for bare
carrier declarations or propositions. Equivalence of propositions is encoded as the
conjunction of two implications. Every source statement is closed and explicitly quantified.

## General theorem coverage

The batch covers eight quotient results: kernel, surjectivity, inclusion–projection
exactness, existence and uniqueness of quotient factorization, both submodule-correspondence
identities, and the embedding of M/ker(f) in the codomain with unchanged image.

Finite-generation cards cover quotients, surjective images, induced submodule carriers,
extensions by a finite quotient, sums and linear images of finitely generated submodules,
tensor products and the middle term of an exact sequence with surjective last map.

Nakayama has three distinct forms: a finitely generated submodule U with I⊆Jac(R) and
U⊆IU must vanish; a finite module M over a local ring with mM=M must vanish; and
U+mM=M implies U=M when M is finite over a local ring. The last form does not assume U
is finite. None of these cards adds a freeness, finite-dimensionality or Noetherian
hypothesis.

Tensoring preserves surjections without flatness. Separate cards retain flatness when
asserting preservation of injections or exact pairs. The profile does not yet include
first-class Hom-module carriers, arbitrary diagrams, tensor universal bilinear maps,
completion, dimension or general algebra-extension structures. These are not implied by
the current cards.

## Bounds and verification

The validator rejects XML over 65,536 UTF-8 bytes, over 4,096 nodes, or deeper than 64
edges. Binders admit 1–256 exact type declarations; conjunction/disjunction admit 2–32
operands. Attributes, namespaces, cdbases, constructor arities, binder order and absence of
shadowed/free variables are checked. Unsupported elements, mixed content and unregistered
symbols fail closed. The registry schema and exact executable symbol inventory are checked
at import; additions do not silently become operators.

Unit tests cover ring/module mismatch, quotient identity, forged sorts, arities, scope,
wrong composition/tensor slots, explicit locality, registry drift, bounds, alpha
canonicalization, exact source serialization and the actual Nakayama assumptions. Authored
hard negatives include counterexamples for missing finite generation, locality, flatness
and quotient factorization hypotheses. They are not a report of runtime retrieval quality.
