# Commutative algebra v3: localization, chain conditions and Hom

`typed-commalg-v3` is an independent, bounded authoring profile. It preserves all existing v1/v2 validators and registries. The 31 general cards quantify over arbitrary commutative rings and modules: 12 chain-condition/finite-length cards, 11 module-localization cards, 5 tensor cards, and 3 Hom cards. No card fixes a numerical exponent or a particular example ring. The paired negative examples deliberately use concrete rings to refute invalid generalizations.

## Scope and status

The profile enables canonical XML validation and locally compiled evidence for Draft authoring. It does not implement model routing, an XML-to-Lean compiler, runtime theorem selection, public publication, or automatic discharge of applicability assumptions. Compiling each Lean witness establishes the Lean theorem, while semantic review separately checks its alignment with the natural statement and XML. The new negative dataset contains mathematical counterexamples and has **not** been run through runtime retrieval or model selection.

Integral extensions, Krull dimension, valuation rings, completion, general local-to-global principles and arbitrary indexed families are outside this profile. Existing v2 quotient, finite-generation and Nakayama cards are not repeated.

## Closed dependent types

Every document starts with explicit quantification. A bound ring has its own identity; `Module(R)`, `Ideal(R)`, `Submonoid(R)`, `Submodule(R,M)` and `LinearMap(R,M,N)` retain the exact ring and carrier. LocalRing is an explicit ring declaration, never inferred merely from the presence of a maximal ideal. Scalar restriction is not implicit between distinct bound rings.

Constructed carriers retain structural operands:

- `quotient(R,M,U)` is `M ⧸ U`; changing U changes the carrier.
- `submodule_carrier(R,M,U)` is the underlying module of U.
- `hom(R,M,N)` is the R-module `M →ₗ[R] N`.
- `tensor(R,M,N)` is `TensorProduct R M N`; swapping operands requires an explicit map.
- `product(R,M,N)` is the componentwise R-module `M × N`.
- `scalar_module(R)` is R itself as its regular module.
- `localized_module(R,M,S)` is `LocalizedModule S M`, regarded as an **R-module**. Both M and the multiplicative submonoid S belong to R. Changing S changes the constructed carrier even if the same module appears.

Constructed carriers cannot be forged as binder declarations. Map equality requires identical source and target sorts. Equality of propositions or carrier declarations is not an operator in the profile. Element variables, scalar variables, unrestricted sets, infinite sequences and arbitrary function application are absent.

## Exact operator meanings

The registry lists every symbol with its ordered signature and mathematical Lean meaning. The executable validator independently pins its complete symbol inventory and registry header, so editing the registry cannot silently authorize another operator.

`compose(R,M,N,P,f,g)` means **g.comp f**; `exact(R,M,N,P,f,g)` means **Function.Exact f g** (range f equals kernel g). Exactness alone does not assert that f is injective or g is surjective. `finite(R,M)` means finite generation (`Module.Finite R M`), not finite cardinality. `fg(R,M,U)` means `U.FG`. `module_zero(R,M)` means `Subsingleton M`. `finite_length` means finite composition length. `noetherian` and `artinian` mean the ascending and descending submodule chain conditions respectively.

`localization_map(R,M,S)` sends x to x/1. `localized_map(R,M,N,S,f)` sends x/s to f(x)/s using canonical localized modules and their common S. `localized_submodule(R,M,S,U)` is `U.localized₀ S (LocalizedModule.mkLinearMap S M)`, all fractions with numerator in U. It is usually larger than the plain image of U under x↦x/1. `ideal_smul(R,M,I,U)` means the R-submodule I•U generated by finite sums of scalar products. In the localization ideal-action card, the same **original R-ideal I** acts on the localized R-module; no unrepresented change of ring is asserted.

`hom_precompose(R,M,N,P,f)` is `Hom(N,P)→Hom(M,P)`, h↦h.comp f, reversing the first argument. `hom_postcompose(R,M,N,P,f)` is `Hom(M,N)→Hom(M,P)`, h↦f.comp h. `tensor_lift(R,M,N,P,b)` takes a bilinear map represented as `M→Hom(N,P)` to its linear tensor factorization; `tensor_curry` reverses this transformation. `bilinear_postcompose(R,M,N,P,Q,b,g)` means b.compr₂ g. The tensor universal-property card asserts existence and uniqueness together, using curry(f)=b as the explicit factorization equation.

The tensor right-exactness and contravariant Hom exactness cards require the terminal map to be surjective. The general module-localization exactness card does not require this. Noetherian Hom requires a finitely generated source and a Noetherian target. These are material theorem assumptions, explicitly retained in all three representations.

## Validation and evidence

The XML allowlist requires the exact OpenMath namespace, exact cdbases and attributes, bound variables, proper binder placement, correct operator arity and compatible dependent carriers. It rejects shadowing, forward references, extra atomic children and mixed text. Bounds are 65,536 UTF-8 bytes, 4,096 XML nodes, depth 64, at most 256 declarations per binder, and logical conjunction/disjunction arity 2–32. Alpha canonicalization is followed by exact-byte validation.

The generator writes the exact concatenated Lean bundle, compiles it using the local pinned workspace, and only then emits JSON. Evidence includes the actual witness, validator and registry SHA-256 digests, toolchain and mathlib revision. The generation command is:

```sh
pals-agent/.venv/bin/python pals-scripts/generate-commalg-v3-batch.py
```

The unit suite checks mismatched rings, multiplicative subsets, localization kernels/ranges, Hom variance, bilinear source order, quotient identities, unbound/forged declarations, namespace/registry drift, bounds, exact witness serialization and critical assumptions. Distinct symbolic subset variables are deliberately not identified by semantic equality: additional transport would require a separately represented proof.
